Research

Persistent plant digital twins via graph memory

Robots that prune or harvest need a plant's structure: which stem carries which leaf, sucker or fruit. But plants are thin, self-occluding and always growing, so every scan is incomplete, and one missed junction detaches a whole branch. I represent each plant as a semantic organ graph and train graph neural networks to repair missing structure, using the repaired graph from the previous visit as memory.

The plant is not the endpoint of the method; it is a challenging deformable, self-occluding physical system for studying structured 3D/4D perception under partial observation, a core problem for Physical AI.

RQ1 · Structural repair. Can topology-aware graph reasoning rebuild a valid plant twin from partial 3D scans better than geometry-only skeletonization?
RQ2 · Living twin. Does memory of the previous twin improve repair, especially where occlusion is heaviest and plants are most complex?

TomatoPGT dataset and tools

Structure-from-motion · organ segmentation · plant graphs

42 annotated 3D point clouds of tomato plants from three cultivars, scanned over 3 months with a turntable photo booth. Organ-level labels, plant graphs and trait tables, plus open tools: CloudSeg for organ segmentation and CloudGraph for point cloud to plant graph.

In progress

Graph-repair GNN for occluded plants

Graph transformers · PyTorch Geometric · GPU training

Graph-transformer GNNs trained to recover removed organs, missing connections and rewired edges, including 3D node positions. The pilot pipeline works end to end; evaluation on the new 13-plant dataset and a temporal version that uses the previous growth stage are next.

Hydroponic harvesting robot

Fusion 360 · electronics · ROS · LiDAR calibration

Designed and built a linear-rail harvesting robot end to end: mechanics, electronics and ROS software. Calibrated its 3-axis arm using a single LiDAR camera.

Hierarchical DDPG for motion planning

Reinforcement learning · manipulator planning · moving obstacles

Implemented and trained Hierarchical DDPG, where a manager agent switches between goal-reaching and collision-avoidance workers. It achieved 38% higher reward and 2× faster learning than single-agent DDPG.
